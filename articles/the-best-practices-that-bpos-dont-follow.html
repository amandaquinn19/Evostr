The Best Practices That BPOs Don't Follow

Coming into BPO from outside the industry, the shock wasn't operational complexity. It was how many go-to-market and business-planning basics — table stakes in other industries — are routinely ignored here.

Leadership Expertise Growth

I didn't come up through the BPO industry. I fell into it somewhat accidentally when the tech company I was working for decided to productize its approach to intelligently routing customer interactions across BPO partners in real time. That business became ArenaCX, the outsourcing marketplace, and I was tapped to lead Finance and Marketing.

From that vantage point, at the intersection of technology, buyers, and BPOs, I got a front-row seat to how this industry actually operates. And I was shocked — not by the complexity of operations or the challenges of workforce management, which I expected, but by how many principles of go-to-market strategy, client relationship management, and business planning I understood as table stakes elsewhere were completely ignored in BPO.

Here are eight of the best practices that keep getting skipped.

Employee Engagement

Communicate transparently and consistently with your team. Not just when there's good news or a crisis, but as a foundational operating rhythm. Information can't be siloed or sporadic if you want real engagement.

Invest in frontline leadership development. Your frontline supervisors oversee 80–90% of your workforce, but in most BPOs they receive minimal training and almost no coaching. If they're not equipped to lead, your entire operation suffers.

Operations & Delivery

Create tight alignment between what sales promises and what ops delivers. The most common client complaint in BPO isn't about quality or pricing — it's "this isn't what we were sold." If your sales team promises proactive insights, how often are you actually delivering them?

Use QBRs as strategic conversations, not tactical report-outs. The best partnerships use quarterly business reviews to understand where clients are going and position the BPO as a strategic partner in getting there. Most BPOs just review last quarter's metrics.

Sales & Marketing

Focus on specific markets or problems to build genuine expertise. Trying to be everything to everyone means you're not actually expert at anything. Specialization makes you more efficient at acquisition and more credible in sales conversations.

Sell solutions and outcomes, not FTEs. Buyers don't care about butts in seats. They care about what those seats will accomplish for their business. If you're still selling labor, you're competing on price by default.

Strategy & Planning

Make planning for the future part of someone's actual role. If everyone is buried in the now, no one is watching the competition, tracking industry shifts, or keeping you ahead of market changes. This can't be a side-of-desk responsibility.

Invest in your business even when ROI isn't immediate. Real investment means betting on things that will pay off in 12–24 months, not just spending on guaranteed quick wins. If you can't do that, you're not investing — you're just maintaining.

Why the Gaps Exist

None of this is about knowledge. Every BPO leader already knows this stuff works. The hard part is execution — and that's the part worth digging into with your leadership team before it shows up as churn or a stalled deal.

When the conversation makes sense, you’ll know. We’ll be here.
