Want More RFP Invitations? Here's the Missing Piece

86% of decision-makers say they're more likely to invite a company to an RFP if it consistently produces high-quality thought leadership. Let that sink in.

Sales

TL;DR

Buyers form opinions about who they trust long before they ever reach out — based on insight and perspective, not sales pitches. The strongest BPOs shape the conversation before the RFP is ever issued.

Before they ever reach out, before they even know they need a new outsourcing partner, buyers are already forming opinions about who they trust. And it's not based on sales pitches or pricing — it's based on insight, expertise, and perspective.

That's why the strongest BPOs don't just deliver great service. They shape the conversation. They make buyers rethink their challenges. They position themselves as the safest, smartest choice before the competition even enters the picture.

What Makes Thought Leadership Effective

According to research, the best content offers a fresh take — it challenges conventional thinking and helps buyers see a better way. It uses strong research and data, because buyers trust insights backed by facts, not opinions. And it provides real guidance — it doesn't just highlight problems, it helps solve them.

FAQ: Earning More RFP Invitations

What counts as "a fresh take" if my industry insights are basically the same data everyone else in BPO cites?

The data can be shared; the interpretation can't be. A fresh take comes from what your operations and client teams see across accounts that a generic industry report doesn't capture — the specific pattern behind the stat, not the stat itself, is what challenges conventional thinking.

How much thought leadership do I need before it actually starts influencing who gets invited to RFPs?

Consistency matters more than volume — a steady drumbeat over two to three quarters builds more buyer recall than a burst of content followed by silence. Buyers are forming an opinion about who they trust well before any RFP exists, so the content needs to be showing up regularly by the time they start that list.

Should this thought leadership go out under the company brand or under my own name as a leader?

Both, carrying the same voice and insight — the company channel builds institutional trust, a named leader's channel builds personal credibility with the buying committee. Pick whichever reaches your audience today and expand from there, but don't let the register drift between the two.
