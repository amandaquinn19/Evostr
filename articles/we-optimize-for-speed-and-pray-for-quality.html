"We Optimize for Speed and Pray for Quality"

The data is in: most BPOs are hiring on hope. Daniel Ash, CEO of Journeyfront, has spent a decade inside BPO hiring processes across 50+ countries — and what he's found is that roughly half of hires are gone within six months. That's not a retention problem. That's a coin flip.

Leadership

If you've ever felt like hiring is a guessing game, this one is for you.

We sat down with Daniel Ash — CEO and co-founder of Journeyfront, an intelligent hiring platform built specifically for BPOs — for an episode of Build a Better BPO. Journeyfront is used by organizations large and small across 50+ countries, and Daniel and his team have spent the last decade working directly inside BPO hiring processes across dozens of clients globally.

That matters, because what he shared wasn't theory. It wasn't a consultant's framework built in a boardroom. It was pattern recognition from someone who has seen what's actually happening on the ground — across geographies, company sizes, and client types — for ten years running.

He described what most BPO hiring processes look like in practice:

"They optimize for speed and pray for quality."

He wasn't being harsh. He was just naming something most of us already know is true but haven't said out loud.

A Systems Failure, Not a Leadership Failure

BPOs face herculean hiring challenges. The pressure to fill a class of 100 by Monday is real. The recruiter managing evergreen reqs across multiple clients, role types, and hiring timelines is doing an impossible job. When accountability is measured by "did you fill the class," quality becomes someone else's problem — and usually, that someone else doesn't show up until two months later, when the attrition numbers come back.

So the speed wins. Every time.

The uncomfortable part? Daniel shared that at many BPOs, roughly 50% of hires are gone within six months. That's not a retention problem. That's a coin flip. And the industry has largely just... accepted it. Built classes around it. Staffed around it. Layered gamification programs and pizza parties on top of it — anything but look at the source.

Hiring and Retention Are One System, Not Two

What struck us most in this conversation was the way Daniel framed hiring and retention as two parts of the same system — and how almost every BPO treats them like completely separate departments with completely separate problems. Retention gets the ping-pong table. Hiring gets the pressure to move fast. Nobody asks whether the person was a fit to begin with.

Daniel also shared something unexpected: some of the things BPO hiring teams trust most — resume experience, phone screens, years in the industry — are often not correlated with job performance. In some cases, they're negatively correlated. The candidates recruiters are most excited about aren't always the ones who stay and perform.

That's not a knock on the recruiting team. That's what happens when a system hasn't been designed to learn from its own outcomes.

The Fix Isn't Magic. It's Structure.

It's deciding upfront what good looks like, building consistent criteria, and using assessments validated against your actual data — meaning you close the loop between who you hired and how they actually performed. Do that, and the coin flip starts looking a lot more like a strategy.

The full episode goes deeper on all of it: the real cost of a bad hire (well over a million dollars a year for even a small org), what great assessments actually measure, how AI is changing hiring and where BPOs should still be skeptical, and what a closed-loop hiring system looks like in practice.

When the conversation makes sense, you’ll know. We’ll be here.
