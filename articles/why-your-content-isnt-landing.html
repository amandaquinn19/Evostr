Why Your Content Isn't Landing (And How to Fix It)

Companies don't read your content. People do. If your messaging is tailored to a generic brand — or a list of industries you serve — you're missing the real decision-maker on the other side.

Sales

Too many BPOs try to market to companies. The problem? Companies don't read your content. People do. When a buyer is choosing a BPO, it's not just a line item on a budget — it's often a career-making or career-breaking decision. That's why you need to speak to them directly, not just pitch your services.

Here's what most BPOs miss. They write copy like: "We serve companies across healthcare, financial services, and e-commerce with solutions spanning back office, customer care, and tech support." But to the head of CX at a high-growth CPG brand, that's meaningless. What they want to hear is: "We help emerging CPG brands retain more customers by modernizing post-purchase support, without adding headcount."

Step 1: Uncover Buyer Motivations (And Fears)

To create buyer-focused content, understand what keeps your prospect up at night: are they worried their current vendor is going to implode during peak season? Under pressure to prove ROI on every vendor decision? Being pushed to adopt AI with no real plan? Worried their team isn't equipped for what's coming, but too stretched to hire fast enough? If your content doesn't address those real concerns, it won't cut through.

Step 2: Build Trust Before the First Call

Most BPOs treat trust-building like a job for the sales team. But trust starts long before your first conversation — a thought-provoking LinkedIn post that makes them feel seen, a blog that unpacks the exact challenge they're trying to solve, a short video showing how you think about AI for their use case. Each piece of content becomes a micro-conversion, a breadcrumb leading them toward "I trust you." By the time they reach out, they're most of the way sold.

The Takeaway

If you want better-fit leads and faster close cycles, stop speaking to "the brand." Speak to the buyer. Make them feel understood, and make them feel like you're already helping, before they ever talk to sales.

FAQ: Writing to the Buyer, Not the Brand

We don't have budget for formal buyer research. How do we find out what's actually on their mind?

Ask your account managers and salespeople what prospects actually say in discovery calls — the objections, the fears, the what-if questions. That's your research; most BPOs already have it and just aren't writing it down.

We sell to several different buyer types. Do we need a separate content stream for each?

You need distinct messaging for each, not a separate content calendar. Same core proof points, reframed around what each buyer specifically cares about — a CFO and a head of CX read the same case study for different reasons.

How much of this kind of content does it actually take before someone reaches out?

There's no fixed number, but the pattern holds — buyers consume several pieces before they ever engage. Consistency over months matters more than any single post; the goal is that they've already decided by the time they need you.
