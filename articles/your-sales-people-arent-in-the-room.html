Your Sales People Aren't in the Room

While your reps discuss CSAT scores with contact center managers, technology vendors are three floors up showing the CFO how to cut interaction costs from $1.87 to $0.38. Same outcome. Different buyer.

Sales AI & Technology

There's a conversation happening in your prospects' C-suite right now. But your sales team isn't in the room.

While your reps are discussing CSAT scores with contact center managers, technology vendors are three floors up, showing the CFO how they can cut interaction costs from $1.87 to $0.38. Same outcome. Different math. And a completely different buyer.

This isn't hypothetical. It's happening right now, and most BPO sales teams have no idea they're already losing deals.

What's Changing

Paul Smith, Principal Consultant and CEO at Smith Slaughter, spent two-thirds of his career in technology sales before moving into BPO, and has watched this shift accelerate over the last 18 months. Among the shifts he's seeing: the "one-hour training trap" that's killing technology offerings before they even launch, compensation models that need to change (a $5,000 tech deal can't compete against a 50-seat labor contract under the same commission structure), and the need to separate your legacy business from your transformation business — because they require different sales talent and different economics entirely.

We know this is uncomfortable territory. Many BPOs built incredible businesses on labor arbitrage and operational excellence, and those skills still matter. But the contact center industry is becoming technology-first. The question isn't whether that shift will happen — it's whether you'll lead it for your clients, or watch them find someone else to guide them through it.

FAQ: Getting Into the Technology Buying Conversation

How do we get access to a CFO conversation we've never been invited to?

You earn the invitation by bringing the CFO's math, not just the ops manager's scorecard — walk in with a cost-per-interaction comparison and a business case, the same artifacts the technology vendors are already using upstairs.

Do we need to hire new salespeople for this, or can we retrain who we have?

Some reps will make the shift with training and new material; others built their entire approach around relationship-based, seat-based selling and won't adapt. Separating legacy and transformation sales talent is the practical answer — you likely need both, not a wholesale replacement.

Should we change compensation before or after we start selling into the C-suite?

Before, if you can. A $5,000 technology deal will always lose to a 50-seat labor contract under the same commission structure, so reps have no incentive to prioritize the conversation you're asking them to have until the comp plan says otherwise.
